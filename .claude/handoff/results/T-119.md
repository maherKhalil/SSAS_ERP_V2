# YOU CAUGHT ME MID-MISTAKE, AND IT WAS ALREADY WRITTEN

**My first implementation was `records = records.Where(inside the window)` — a blanket filter over the record
set.** It was one line shorter and it silently made a second, opposite decision.

```
UnpaidAbsenceQuantity   a DEDUCTION   excluding it protects the employee   <- RULED, and all this does
WorkedQuantity          hourly PAY    excluding it WITHHOLDS money
OvertimeQuantityByTier  overtime PAY  excluding it WITHHOLDS money
PaidAbsenceQuantity     never reaches Payroll
```

**Narrowed to the deduction alone.** The window is computed once and applied to one sum.

## The asymmetry is real, and it is why the ruling only covered the deduction

**Absence outside employment is NOISE** — a stale record for somebody who had left, meaning nothing.
**Work outside employment is a CONTRADICTION** — either they worked, so the termination date is wrong, or
they did not, so the record is.

**Silently resolving a contradiction either way moves money on data the product knows is inconsistent.**

# ⚠ WHAT I DID NOT DECIDE, AND WHY IT IS YOURS

**Worked hours and overtime recorded outside the employment window are still counted, unchanged.**

**My recommendation is to REFUSE THE RUN**, and the precedent is this product's own: `DailySalaryHasNoWorkingDays`
fails the whole run rather than one employee, because *"a run that silently omitted one person's pay would be
discovered on payday."* **Contradictory employment data is the same shape — visible wrong beats invisible
wrong.**

**I did not build it, for two reasons.** It needs a refusal path the summary does not have — the contract
reports values, not failures, so it would mean a new `AttendanceSummaryStatus` or a Payroll-side check —
**and that is a contract decision rather than a filter placement.** And the argument for it is mine, not
ruled: **leaving them unfiltered is defensible on the grounds you named, that refusing to pay recorded hours
resolves contradictory data in the employer's favour.**

**Left explicitly counted rather than accidentally counted**, with the reasoning at the site.

## And a write-time guard would not have delivered the ruling

**Termination is routinely entered after the fact — terminate on the 20th effective the 15th — and the
records are already written by then.** A write-time refusal cannot see a termination that has not happened
yet.

**The summary is computed when the run is calculated, so it sees both orderings.** A guard catching only one
ordering, where the other is the normal one, would have looked like coverage.

## Why Attendance rather than Payroll

**The summary returns totals, and a total cannot be narrowed after the fact** — the same wall T-115 hit.
Payroll's alternative meant putting the employment window into the contract, **which is the employee
dimension guard 3 exists to keep out.** `AttendanceSummaryResult` is unchanged and guard 3 never fired.
