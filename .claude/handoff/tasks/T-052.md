## Scope

Fixed-asset domain model and the application layer over it:

- **Asset**, its acquisition, its cost basis, its useful life and its disposal.
- **Depreciation**: at minimum straight-line; whatever else the business rules require. The
  schedule is domain logic, belongs in `.Domain`, and is unit-tested without a database.
- **Application handlers** for the commands and queries the module owns.
- **`SSAS.Assets.Contracts`**: only what a consumer *outside* the module needs. Nothing
  speculative — `SSAS.GL.Contracts.csproj` records why: *"that is the difference between a
  contract and a wish."*

Read `docs/00-Master-Product-Specification/Business-Rules.md` before designing anything, and cite
the rules you implement. Where the specification is silent on a case you must decide, **decide it,
record it as an open decision in your result, and state the assumption** — do not invent a rule
silently, and do not block on it.

## Posting to the ledger

Assets will eventually post depreciation journals. **The port already exists**:
`SSAS.GL.Contracts.Posting.IJournalPoster`, in an assembly that holds no project references on
purpose. You may reference that assembly and depend on the interface.

You may **not** edit anything under `src/Modules/Finance/`. If `IJournalPoster`'s shape does not
fit depreciation posting, that is a genuine finding and a real possibility — the interface was
shaped by Payroll, its own comments say so, and it has never had a second consumer. **Report the
mismatch precisely; do not widen the interface.** Being the second consumer is how a port learns
it was over-fitted to the first, and that finding is worth more than a workaround.
