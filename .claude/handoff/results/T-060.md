## Why the instrument changed and not the corpus

`BRULE-XX-NNNN` and `BR-XX-NNNN` are **different rules that share a number** — nine for nine in FP-001,
twelve for twelve in FP-006 — and `BRULE-` is cited in 86 files under `src/` and `tests/`. A rename
would have collapsed rules and **turned this checker green by destroying content.**

The gate was **wrong about a fact**; a failing build is a failing build under any convention, so the
instrument had to change. Here **the corpus is coherent and the checker did not know one of its
conventions.** Opposite treatment, and arguing both is not inconsistency.

## The divergence is declared and loud, never absorbed

```
Convention: LEGACY — BRULE- homed in business-rules.md, BR- in requirements.md (a SEPARATE space, not a renaming)
Convention: MODERN — BR- homed in business-rules.md, no BRULE- space
Convention: UNDECLARED — this package is in no PACKAGE_CONVENTION entry
```

Printed for **every package, every run, pass or fail.** The convention is declared in a table — never
inferred, and **never a fallback that searches the other home when a lookup misses**, which is the
silent-permissive failure removed from four instruments on 2026-08-27.

## Two regressions my own change caused, found by running it

**Neither was in the plan and both were caught by the before/after table**, not by reading.

**FP-009 went 9 → 11.** Adding `BRULE-` as a space subjected it to the contiguity rule, and **FP-009
numbers its document rules in an 0600 block** — `BRULE-DOC-0601`, `0602`, `0604`, `0606`. The first run
demanded **600 missing identifiers that were never meant to exist.**

**FP-010 went GREEN → RED.** I declared it `legacy` and the conformance check demanded a `BRULE-`
definition. **FP-010 has no `business-rules.md` at all** — it holds `README.md`, `carried-analysis.md`
and `decisions-open.md`. A package with no home file cannot violate home-file placement.

Both fixed: **`BRULE-` is exempt from contiguity** — reported as a warning, never failed — and
conformance only fires when the home file exists.

**The exemption is not a weakening.** Contiguity was never asserted for `BRULE-` because the space did
not exist in this checker until today; asserting it now would be **inventing a rule nobody wrote.**
The numbering stays visible as a warning until someone establishes what it means.

### The matrix question, established: the parser is narrow AND the documents are heterogeneous

The parser requires **a row of at least four cells whose first cell carries a `REQ-` identifier.**

```
package                            cols   first-cell space
FP-001-identity-access                3   DEC-
FP-002-authentication-token-life      5   none
FP-003-tenant-lifecycle               7   none
FP-004-localization                   4   BR-
FP-005-company-legal-entity           7   none
FP-006-hr-employee                    7   none
FP-008-hr-position                    3   REQ-      <- has the anchor, too few columns
FP-009-hr-employee-import-export      2   REQ-      <- has the anchor, too few columns
FP-014-subscription (parses)          4   REQ-
```

**Eight failing matrices, eight different shapes** — 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 columns, first cells carrying
`DEC-`, `BR-`, `REQ-` or nothing. **This is not one defect with one fix**, and it is exactly why the
instruction was to establish before reformatting. Under the legacy convention the chain is anchored by
`DEC-`/`BR-` rather than `REQ-`, so **the parser encodes the modern shape** — but that alone does not
explain FP-008 and FP-009, which carry the anchor and are simply too narrow.

**Not fixed here, deliberately.** Changing chain parsing alters coverage rules 3 and 4 across **every**
package including the four that are green, which is a far larger blast radius than the home-file
change. It is its own task with its own before/after.
