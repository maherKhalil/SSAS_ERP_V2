## I was wrong about the scope, in the direction I had warned about

I told the architect the number was **a floor**. It was **a ceiling**.

The reasoning was: *FP-014 is not obviously special, so the other thirteen packages are
unchecked.* True as far as it went, and it skipped the cheaper question — **whether the other
packages are even SHAPED so the defect is possible.** Eleven of them are not, and one `ls`

**That is the same error this loop has made repeatedly today from four directions: inferring
scope from the symptom rather than from what the symptom is made of.**

**And the caution was still right to raise.** The answer had to be measured, and building the
checker produced a number where either of us would otherwise have produced an estimate. A
caution that turns out to be wrong is not the same as one that should not have been raised —
the reasoning was unfounded and the decision it produced was correct, and those are separable.

## The marker vocabulary is surveyed, not invented — which corrects something I wrote

**My first draft of the check's header stated that no open register in this repository had ever
marked a decision ruled, and built the vocabulary as declared-rather-than-discovered on that
basis. It was false.**

**FP-013 has carried the convention since its ratification** — `**RULED: <the answer>**` under
all sixteen of its headings. So `RULED` is taken from the corpus, and **my own `— **RULED**`
marker in T-036 matched an existing convention by coincidence rather than by looking.**

The check accepts the marker **in the heading or beneath it**, because the two packages place it
differently — FP-013 on the line below, FP-014's single marked decision in the heading itself.
**Neither placement is adjudicated.** Reporting the divergence is this check's job; choosing a
house style is not, and a checker enforcing a style nobody ruled becomes an argument rather than
a tool.

Word forms match **uppercase only**: lower-case "ruled" is ordinary prose in these files
(*"`OD-SUB-0009` ruled that…"*) and matching it would make every cross-reference look like an
acknowledgement. **The check prints which marker it matched**, so the vocabulary is auditable
from the output rather than trusted.

## What it cannot see — printed on every run, green or red

> *A ruling recorded in NEITHER register — in a note or a message — is invisible here and to
> check 6 alike. A GREEN means the two registers agree, NOT that every decision is recorded
> somewhere.*

`DEC-L-034` was exactly that until T-036 moved it, which is why the sentence is in the output
and not only in the header. Further limits, in the header:

- it reads **headings**, so a decision discussed in prose without one is unseen;
- it cannot tell a **stale** acknowledgement from a current one — a heading marked RULED whose
  ruling was later reversed reads as agreement;
- a package with no `decisions-open.md` is **skipped, not passed**, and the output says so per
  package rather than silently omitting them.

Its bias is to **over-report**, which is this script's stated posture: a checker that
under-reports is worse than one that occasionally asks a human to look.
