## What is in it

Five decisions: the boundary; `ADR-001` standing unamended; **what the product still owns** — invoice,
payment flow, outcome and reconciliation, everything except the instrument, so `OD-SUB-0016` is
satisfied in substance; the capability rule; and the **deliberate absence of a provider choice**,
which is commercial and not architecture's to make.

Consequences are stated in both directions. The negatives are real and named: no in-page card form of
the product's own design, **a provider outage is a payment outage with no fallback** — that is what
*ever* costs — and provider migration is more than configuration because tokens do not port.

The risks section says where the rule will actually erode, which is not where anyone would look:

> Nobody will propose a `Pan` column. The realistic failure is a free-text `Notes`, `Description` or
> `Metadata` field on a payment type, into which a support process pastes a card number. Decision 4's
> capability test is the mitigation and it is weakest exactly here, because such a field is capable by
> accident.

Plus: a provider callback logged verbatim during an incident, and the observation that **"tokenized"
is not self-defining** — a reversible encoding of the PAN would satisfy the word and violate the
decision, so the test is whether the value is meaningless outside the provider.

`# What this ADR does not decide` names provider, settlement, refunds, credit notes, dunning,
chargebacks, tax jurisdiction, and which module owns the payment surface — each unauthored today.

**PCI-DSS is not restated.** The standard is named twice, both times as scope this product stays
outside of. No summary, nothing to rot.
