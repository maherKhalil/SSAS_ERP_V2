## What was actually missing — narrower than the task assumed, and worth recording

**`TenantDbContext` already documented more than expected.** Its existing comment explains the
dispatch mechanism and even records the historical defect: *"overriding only the convenience
overload — as this type previously did — left `SaveChangesAsync(true, ct)` able to commit against a
frozen tenant."*

**So the gap was not the mechanism. It was that the comment explained why the type does not *need* a
convenience overload, and never said it may not *have* one.** A future editor reading it would learn
that the lock is taken once and conclude that a delegating overload is harmless — it takes no second
lock.

The new block makes that the separate point it is, and names the reason, which is **not** the lock:

> **An outer overload is a place a future rule gets written**, and a rule written there is reachable
> past by any caller naming the inner one — exactly how seven `PlatformDbContext` guards sat
> bypassable until T-015. Declaring no such method removes the place rather than relying on nobody
> using it.

It cites the guard by full method name, so an editor meets what will stop them rather than discovering
it in CI.

## The guard now names both contexts
