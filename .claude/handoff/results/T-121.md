# THE RULING, BUILT

```
AttendanceSummaryStatus.EmploymentDataContradictory = 4
Payroll.AttendanceContradictsEmployment  ->  409
```

**The summary reports the contradiction; the calculate handler refuses the run before any quantity is
read.** Placed before the four reads deliberately: **each would otherwise turn a contradiction into a zero,
which is indistinguishable from an employee who simply did nothing. A refusal names the employee; a zero
does not.**

## Why a status and not a filter, in one line

**T-119 dropped ABSENCE outside the window; T-121 refuses WORK outside it, and they are the same window
applied to opposite kinds of fact.** Absence outside employment is noise. **Work outside employment means
one of two facts is wrong** — either they worked and the termination date is wrong, or they did not and the
record is — **and nothing here can tell which.**

## And it is the first employee dimension in this contract

**It enters as a STATUS rather than a quantity**, which is what keeps it legitimate under the rule guard 3
enforces: **a status cannot encode a ratio, a schedule or a policy.** It says only that this employee's data
disagrees with itself. **Guard 3 did not fire** — `AttendanceSummaryResult`'s components are unchanged.

## What the register sees, and it is not an accident

`AttendanceContradictsEmployment` is returned by `CalculatePayrollRunCommandHandler`, **which is a seed** —
so the error-mapping register saw it immediately and would have failed had I not mapped it. **Contrast
`OneOffPaymentElementNotPayable`, returned by the static calculator, which was invisible for three tasks
until T-120 seeded it.** The difference is where the `return` sits, not how careful anybody was.
