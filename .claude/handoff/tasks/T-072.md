# T-072 — The join that nothing asserts: an endpoint's permission must be one the catalog defines

**You named this yourself at the end of T-071, and you named it better than I framed it. It is the task.**

## What is actually missing

Three links, and I verified the middle one is absent rather than assuming it:

| Asserted | Where | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| a route requires the permission the **inventory** names | `PayrollRouteInventoryTests.cs:74-84` | HR, GL, Payroll |
| the **contributor's** names reach the composed catalog | `EmployeeHostCompositionTests.cs` H11 | **HR only** |
| the permission a **route requires** is one the **catalog defines** | **nowhere** | — |

**You suspected the third was absent and said you had not established it. I established it.** I swept
every test file touching `IPermissionCatalog`, `ComposedPermissionCatalog` or
`PermissionCatalogContributor`, then filtered to those also touching endpoints or policy names. **One
candidate survived — `EmployeeHostCompositionTests.cs` — and reading it shows H11 iterates the
contributor and asserts each name is grantable.** It never looks at a route. Your suspicion was right
and is now a finding.

**Two facts make this FP-015's problem specifically:** Attendance has **no route inventory test at
all**, and H11 covers **HR only**. Both modules this package touches sit outside both surviving guards.

## The failure mode, which is the reason this outranks the grammar divergence

`AuthorizationNameValidator.cs` accepts **any** segment count; `PermissionName.cs:36` requires exactly
three. So an endpoint can require a name the catalog can never define.

- **Contributed as well** → startup throw. Loud, immediate, safe. Not the problem.
- **Required at an endpoint, never contributed** → **403 to every caller, forever.** Green build,
  green suite, and no test travels the path, because tests mint permission claims directly rather than
  going through assignment.

**That is FP-006P verbatim.** And your own correction to my framing is the sharp version: the route
inventory catches a route requiring the *wrong* permission, so what survives is **the inventory and
the endpoint agreeing with each other while both disagree with the catalog.** Three places, two of
which are the same document.

## Build the join

**Assert, from the production composition: every permission any endpoint requires is defined by the
composed catalog.**

**Enumerate the endpoints, do not list them.** `EmployeeHostCompositionTests` already builds the real
Host graph — that is the pattern to follow. **A hand-written list of expected permissions would be a
third document that can agree with the other two while all three disagree with the catalog**, which
is the exact defect being closed. Recover the required names from the endpoints' authorization
metadata via `PermissionAuthorizationDefaults.TryGetPermissionName` (`:20-37`), which exists for
precisely this and is the inverse of what the endpoints used to construct them.

**Module-agnostic by construction.** Written this way it covers Attendance without Attendance needing
a route inventory, and it covers the next module before anyone remembers to add one.

## What it must handle, and what it must not

- **Anonymous or non-permission endpoints are not failures.** Health, auth, whatever else. Skip them
  by the absence of a permission requirement — **and report the count skipped**, because a filter that
  silently skips everything is a guard that passes on an empty set.
- **Do not assert a count of endpoints.** That is a different guard, it churns on every route added,
  and it is not what is missing.
- **Do not narrow `AuthorizationNameValidator`.** Reconciling the two grammars is a real question and
  a separate decision — narrowing the endpoint side would change what `PermissionRequirement` accepts
  across the whole surface. **Report whether you think it is additionally warranted once the join
  exists; do not do it.** With the join in place the divergence is caught rather than fatal, which may
  be the whole answer.
- **Do not add, rename or remove any permission.** Nothing in FP-015 gets built by this task.

## Prove it can fail — and this one has a specific trap

**Plant the failure per `DEC-L-059a`.** But note what the honest plant is: an endpoint requiring a
name no contributor defines. **A four-segment name is the wrong plant** — it would fail at
`PermissionName.Create` and you would be demonstrating the grammar, not the join. **Plant a
well-formed three-segment name that no contributor defines.** That is the case that reaches production
today and answers 403 forever.

**If the guard cannot be made to go red by that plant, the guard is wrong** — and you found a check
that could not fail eight days of findings ago, so you know the shape.
