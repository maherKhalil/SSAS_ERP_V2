**Establish only. Nothing routed, nothing fixed.**

serve**. The question was whether FP-001's seventeen are engineering's to take or the owner's to unblock.
Answering it for FP-001 made the other four packages cheap to answer too, so all 67 are classified here.

## FP-001's seventeen, which was the question

Thirteen are the administrative surfaces **owner decision #2 already names**: *"tenants, roles, and users
beyond de/reactivation."* Six user routes, seven role routes. Three more are support-plane variants of the
same user routes. **So sixteen of the seventeen are decision #2**, and that decision's options are build,
build a slice, or defer knowingly — a scope and cost call, not a design unknown.

**The seventeenth is unblocked.** `GET /api/platform/permissions` reads the permission catalogue.
`ListPermissionCatalogQueryHandler` exists, `Platform.Permissions.View` is already catalogued, and a
read of a static catalogue changes nothing and costs nothing to decide. It is routing work and no more.

### Decision #2's own numbers, verified rather than quoted

It claims *"Platform permissions catalogued 28, of those required by no route 16."* Measured today against
the running application's route table: **28 catalogued, 12 required by a live route, 16 required by none.**
The decision is accurate.

Those 16 map onto the unrouted rows exactly — `Permissions.View` for the one unblocked read, `Roles.*` and
`RolePermissions.*` for the seven role routes, `Users.*` and `UserRoles.*` for the six user routes, and
`Tenants.*` for FP-003 rather than FP-001. **The permissions were designed and the handlers were built; only
the transport is missing**, which is what makes #2 a cost decision rather than a design one.

## ⚠ Ten rows are not gaps, and the documents already say so

Four in FP-001 and five in FP-007 document a path that was **built under a different one**, and each row
carries the real path in its own notes:

```
POST /api/platform/users/{}/deactivate     BUILT as .../tenant-users/{id}/deactivation
POST /api/platform/me/select-tenant        BUILT as POST /api/platform/auth/select-tenant
GET  /api/platform/me/tenant-memberships   SERVED BY THE LOGIN RESPONSE
POST /api/hr/departments/{}/parent         built as /move and /move-to-root
PUT  /api/hr/departments/{}/manager        built as POST /manager and /manager/remove
POST /api/hr/departments/{}/reactivate     built as /activate
```

**These inflate the capability gap by 10 while describing capability that exists.**

## ⚠ CORRECTED AFTER FIRST WRITING: IT IS TEN AND ONE, NOT NINE AND TWO

This report first said nine drift rows and two unblocked items, naming
`POST /api/hr/employees/{id}/department` as genuinely new **because the row begins "New."**. It also ends
`[BUILT as POST /api/hr/employees/{id}/change-department]`, shipped 2026-08-22 under `DEC-DEP-0024`'s
named-POST convention.

**So this report fell into the trap it describes, in the paragraph above the one describing it.** The
general form needs a second clause: *an instrument that reads only the machine-readable columns will miss
the corrections people wrote in the margin — and so will a reader who stops at the first word.*

The count was caught by a question, not by an instrument: being asked to name the second of the two.

## ⚠ AND THE TEN SHOULD NOT BE EDITED

The first version of this report recommended rewriting the ten paths to the real routes. **That was wrong
and is withdrawn.**

Every one of the ten carries `[BUILT as <real route>]` **and a decision reference justifying the
difference** — `DEC-DEP-0023` for `/move` and `/move-to-root`, `DEC-DEP-0024` for the named-POST
convention, `DEC-DEP-0025` for `/activate`. The rows record **what was specified, what was built, and why
they differ.** Rewriting the path column would delete the first and third of those to fix a count.

**The documents are not wrong. The instrument was.** It compared method-and-path and ignored a marker the
documents use consistently and explain in their own legend. Filtering on that marker takes 67 to 42 in one
line — the 41 owner-blocked rows plus the one that is ours.

## FP-003's fifteen were already decided

Fourteen carry `[DEFERRED - AC-TEN-0020]` in the document itself; the fifteenth is
`DELETE /api/platform/tenants/{}`, marked `[SUPERSEDED - no delete exists, by DEC-TEN-0007]` — a route that
will never be built. **These were never an undiscovered gap.** Decision #2 already notes that a tenants
slice is separately blocked by this deferral.

## What this means for the owner's page

The page currently says the capability gap is 67 rows. The honest sentence is:

> **41 rows wait on five decisions that are yours. 15 are already deferred by a record you accepted. 10
> describe capability that exists under a different path, and our count did not read the note saying so. One
> is ours and small.**

The five decisions, with what each holds: **#11** the commercial plane (24 rows), **#2** administration
transport (16), **#5** attendance bulk import (1). #3 and #4 block work but no contract rows.

## My judgement

**Do not edit the ten.** They are correct and they carry history a rewrite would destroy. Correct the
COUNT instead — one marker filter — and the framing, which this report now carries.

**Do not build the sixteen.** Decision #2 is a real cost decision and the owner has not made it.

**Take the one unblocked row opportunistically**, not as a task of its own: the permissions catalogue read
is one route over an existing handler with an existing permission.
