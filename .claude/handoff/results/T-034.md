## The design, and why the obvious fix was the wrong one

You offered three options — a shared composition helper, an extension the hosts call, or a startup
guard. **All three sit on the registration side, and that is where the original defect lives.** A helper
callers must remember to call is one more step a sixth host can omit in silence: the fix and the bug are
the same shape.

So the obligation sits at the **declaration site**. Each of the ten `Map*Endpoints` entry points calls
`RequireModuleEnablementServices(<module>.Key)`, which refuses to map without the contract registered.
**A host cannot forget a check that is not its to make** — mount a module's routes and the obligation
comes with them; mount none and you are never asked.

**Presence is probed, not resolved.** `ITenantModuleEntitlement` is scoped; resolving it from the root
provider would throw for the wrong reason under scope validation, and a guard that fails for the wrong
reason is worse than none because it teaches people to ignore it. `IServiceProviderIsService` answers
the question actually being asked without constructing anything.

**It beats the `IPermissionCatalogContributor` precedent rather than matching it.** That one is loud only
because the Host eagerly composes the catalog at startup; this one cannot be composed lazily at all.

**Stated as a limit rather than left implied:** this move needs a declaration site every caller must pass
through. Where none exists, it is unavailable, and the honest fallback is the loud-at-startup version.
