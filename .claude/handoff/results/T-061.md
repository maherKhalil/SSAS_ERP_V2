### How the dry run was built, so it can be disbelieved precisely

It **imports `scripts/trace-check.py` unchanged and replaces only `parse_chain`.** Every rule that
produced the numbers above is the shipped rule; the single variable is the parser. The widened parser

identifier, and finds the criteria and test cells **by content rather than by position** — the shipped
parser reads criteria from cell 2 and tests from cell 3, and the real tables put them in columns 3
through 6.

## The eight matrices, classified by reading them — not by column count

**The unit is the TABLE, not the package.** Every matrix file holds several tables of different kinds,
and the parser looks for one shape across all rows of a file.

```

FP-001         1              1    Decision / requirement | Acceptance criteria | Test scenarios
FP-002         4              3    Capability | Business and functional req | Security and NFR | AC | TS
FP-003         5              5    Capability | Business req and rules | Functional and security | NFR | AC | TS
FP-004         6              4    Theme | Requirements/rules | Acceptance | Tests | Decisions
FP-005         2              1    Capability | Business req and rules | ... | AC | TS

FP-008         7              5    Rule | Decision | Acceptance criteria | Test scenarios | Status
FP-009        19              6    Requirement | Rules | Criteria | Scenarios | Determined by
```

**All eight are legacy-shape-to-parse, not deliberate-mapping-to-declare.** Every one contains at
least one table pairing criteria with scenarios. What made them unparseable is the shipped parser's
two assumptions — **a `REQ-` anchor in cell 1, and criteria/tests at fixed column offsets** — and the
legacy tables satisfy neither.

### Correcting the premise I supplied for this task

**My T-060 column-count table was the wrong instrument**, and the architect's caution about it was
right. `FP-004` looked like a 4-column `BR-`-anchored chain; **its first table is a family inventory**
(`Family | Defined range | Count | Authority`) and its chains are in later tables. `FP-008` and
`FP-009` looked "too narrow"; their first tables are source-requirement coverage maps and their chains
are in tables 2 onward. **The column count describes the first table in a file that has up to
nineteen.**

### And FP-007's declared gap is inaccurate

FP-007 is green with a declared gap saying its matrix *"maps requirements to code symbols and test
class names rather than to AC-/TS- identifiers."* **Its matrix contains four chain-bearing tables and
24 rows pairing an `AC-` with a `TS-`.** The statement is true of its *first* table and false of the
file. **The declaration is doing real work — it is why FP-007 is green — and it is describing
something other than what it appears to describe.**

## What I recommend, and I am not building it

**Do not widen the parser alone.** On this measurement that trades 29 failures for 93, of which 55 are
false and 55 land on two packages that are correctly specified.

Three separable pieces, in this order:

1. **Rule 4 must look where coverage is actually recorded** — `test-scenarios.md` as well as the
   matrix. That alone removes 55 of the 64, and it is a rule fix rather than a parser fix.
2. **Rule 4 must expand ranges**, which check 5 already parses. Removes 1.
3. **Then** widen the parser, which by then reveals **13 real gaps and nothing else.**

**Piece 1 before piece 3.** In the other order, a run that finds thirteen genuine tracing gaps arrives
buried in fifty-five false ones, on two packages whose specifications are fine — and the most likely
response to that is to stop trusting the checker.
