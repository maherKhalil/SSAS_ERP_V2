fire when `PayrollEmployeeInput`'s component set changes. **`DEC-L-069`, mine: I scoped the search to where

**The guard is a tripwire and it worked exactly as designed.** Its header:

> *"They fail on ANY change to the shapes they assert, not only on employment type. So a message must state
> the question, never the cause."*

**So I answered its question in the guard, rather than loosening it:**

- **`WorkedQuantity` is not a ratio, a schedule or a policy.** It is hours actually attended — an *observed*
  quantity, never a contractual dimension. A 0.6 FTE employee and a full-timer who took two weeks off
  produce the same number, **which is exactly why it cannot express what this guard watches for.**
- **And it NARROWS what the guard says is at stake, for one type.** An `Hourly` employee's pay is
  `rate × hours`, so a part-timer is paid correctly **by construction** and no human has to encode a ratio.
  `Monthly` is unchanged and the guard's original paragraph still describes it exactly.
- **The assertion stays exact-equality.** Relaxing it to "contains" would answer the question once and never
  ask again, which is the entire value of a tripwire.

**This is the one existing test that needed editing, and it is not one of the fourteen.**

**Eight new tests in a new file**, so neither neighbour had to be edited. Each is arithmetic chosen so that
**assuming the wrong unit gives a different number:**

```
An_hourly_salary_is_the_rate_times_the_hours_attended        20 x 160 = 3200; as DAYS it would be 400
An_hourly_salary_is_not_prorated_for_a_mid_period_joiner     hired on the 24th, still 800
```

**And the pair on the absence quantity carries a limitation I wrote into the file rather than leaving
implied:** the monthly case would pass under either unit, because 5 days at 100 and 5 hours at 100 are the
same arithmetic. **The hourly case carries the unit; the monthly case carries the price. Neither proves it
alone**, and a reader who assumed one test did would be wrong.
