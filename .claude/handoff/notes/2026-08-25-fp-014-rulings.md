## The gateable surface, now that `OD-SUB-0005` is ruled

Seventeen route groups is the wrong number for scoping the retrofit. They partition as:

| | Groups | Gated? |
|---|---|---|
| Host | 1 | **exempt** — `REQ-SUB-0013` |
| Platform (auth, support auth, localization, identity/access, support authority, company) | 6 | **exempt** — `REQ-SUB-0013` |
| HR | 7 | gated, **one** module key |
| Finance/GL | 1 | gated |
| Payroll | 1 | gated |
| Attendance | 1 | gated |

**Ten route groups across four module keys.** The retrofit is materially smaller than the raw count
suggested, and the exempt seven are exactly the surface that must stay reachable so a disabled tenant
can still authenticate and be re-enabled.

## ⚠ Consequence of `OD-SUB-0016` — flagged once, then built as ruled

Issuing invoices **and** capturing payment in-product is the largest commitment in this package, and it
reaches past FP-014:

- **PCI-DSS scope.** Handling cardholder data brings the components that touch it into scope for
  assessment. The standard containment answer is to isolate the cardholder-data environment from the
  application — which sits in tension with `ADR-001 Modular Monolith`. **This needs an ADR that either
  qualifies `ADR-001` for this one boundary or rules that capture is tokenized such that no cardholder
  data enters the monolith.** It is not an FP-014 model-document decision; it is architectural, and I
  will raise it as its own ADR task rather than let a package settle it implicitly.
- **Tax.** Multi-currency invoicing from vendor to tenant crosses jurisdictions. Which tax regime
  applies to each tenant is unauthored anywhere in this repository.
- **Dunning, refunds and reconciliation** become product surfaces with no requirement covering them
  today — `REQ-SUB-0025` and `REQ-SUB-0026` are one line each.

None of this reverses the ruling. It is recorded so the model documents carry it and so the ADR gets
written before code, not after.

# Architect rulings — round 3, 2026-08-25

Taken as architect rather than escalated. Each is reversible by the owner; none is a guess.

## `OD-SUB-0017` residue — what happens when a seat cap is exceeded

**Ruled: the cap is enforced at admission, not at login. Nobody already working is ever locked out.**

The question as posed — block the next login like a licence, or bill an overage and lock nobody out —
is a false pair, and both answers are wrong for this product.

- **Enforcement point is the grant.** Creating or activating a `TenantUser` beyond the tenant's seat
  cap is **refused at that moment**, with an error naming the cap, the current count and the plan.
  The person who caused the excess is the person who is told, immediately, and they can act on it.
- **Login is never refused for a seat cap.** Blocking a login enforces against an arbitrary user who
  did nothing, at the moment they sit down to work, in an ERP of record. That converts a commercial
  disagreement into an operational outage for someone with no ability to resolve it.
- **An excess that arrives by another route is billed, never enforced retroactively.** Additive-only
  grants (`OD-SUB-0011`) mean a grant cannot lower a cap, so the remaining route is a plan change.
  Where a downgrade would put a tenant over its new cap, the overage is billed and reported; existing
  users keep working.

**Grace period: none, and none is needed.** A grace period exists to soften a lapse. Nothing lapses
here — the refusal is at admission and it is immediate, specific and actionable. `OD-SUB-0009`'s
expiry is the only commercial event that blocks login, and it blocks the whole tenant on a dated,
foreseeable term rather than an arbitrary user on an incremental one. That asymmetry is deliberate.

## `Platform.` prefix ambiguity — the coder's mitigation, extended

**Ruled: keep the prefix; make the plane mechanical with an architecture guard; and guard the
existing names, not only the new six.**

A second naming scheme for seven permissions is worse than one ambiguous scheme, and renaming
permissions already assigned to real roles is a migration that buys nothing functional. But guarding
only `Platform.Plans.*`, `Platform.Subscriptions.*`, `Platform.EntitlementGrants.*` and
`Platform.Invoices.*` would leave the ambiguity that already exists unguarded — `Platform.Support.*`
is platform-plane today and nothing asserts it. **The guard covers the whole platform-plane set.**

currently changing. Paying it alongside work that is already in the file is how it actually lands.
