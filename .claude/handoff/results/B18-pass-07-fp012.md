## ⚠ ON THE FLOORS — THE LIMIT IS RIGHT AND IT CHANGES HOW I CITE THEM

The ruling's addition: **a floor catches the COLLAPSE, not the DRIFT.** `strings.Length >= 12` still passes
if a reflection query silently returns thirty of forty columns.

⚠ **So I have stopped describing them as controls and started describing them as what they are: proof the
query found SOMETHING.** In pass 06 I wrote *"a floor of 12 columns is its control"* — **that overstates
it, and the same sentence would have been wrong about a drifting query.** **The pass-06 citations stand,
the characterisation does not, and I am recording the correction rather than editing the comments to hide
it.**

**The genuinely strong controls I have seen remain the two-sided ones**: FP-006's revocation trio
(*succeed, revoke, fail*) and `AC-PAY-0004` (*stored **and** flagged **and** surfaced*) — **each fails if
either side moves.**
