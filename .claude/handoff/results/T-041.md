## What was built

`TermDays = 14`, the `XXX` currency, the reason codes and the module list. `TrialSubscriptionSeed.Sql`

## Criterion 3 — the trial is the plan, and nothing was invented

No `IsTrial`, no fourth lifecycle value, no flag, no branch anywhere asking whether a subscription is a
trial. The trial is `SubscriptionPlan` `TRIAL-14D` plus `SubscriptionTerm.Fixed(now, now + 14d)`.

**The consequence is what makes the ruling pay:** the day a customer buys, the only act is appending a
record naming a different plan. Nothing is cleared, transitioned or migrated — the trial ends because a
later record took effect, which is the mechanism every plan change already uses.

Asserted as a **tripwire** rather than a comment: a reflective sweep over `TenantSubscription`,
`SubscriptionPlan`, `SubscriptionPlanStatus` and `TenantStatus` fails if any member's name contains
"Trial". It checks names, not intent — what it stops is the ordinary version of the mistake, the
convenience column added because a query was awkward.

## Criterion 4 — running it twice issues once, and the test proves the right thing

**In `Platform.Tests`, the clock advances between the two runs.** With a frozen clock the second append
would be refused by `NonMonotonicAppend` — the right outcome for entirely the wrong reason, and the test
would have looked green forever while asserting nothing about double-issuing. Moving the clock makes the
second append perfectly legal, so the refusal can only be the tenant-already-holds-a-record rule.

**In `Integration.Tests`, the same statement the migration ships is executed twice against real SQL.**
Three tenants, seeded, seeded again: the count is unchanged **and so is every `EffectiveFromUtc`**. The
second assertion is the one that matters — a duplicate would not have to change the total to do damage,
because the record in force is the one with the greatest `EffectiveFromUtc`.

**And the case the guard actually exists for has its own test in both suites:** a tenant already on a
purchased plan. A trial appended afterwards takes effect later and becomes the record in force — a paying
customer silently downgraded to a 14-day trial, with no error anywhere. That is not the case the criterion
described, and it is the expensive one.

## The interaction with the `THROW`, stated from both ends

`AddSubscriptionCommercialPlane` ends by counting plans, subscriptions and grants and **throwing if any
exist**. The seed writes all three. Both are correct:

- the `THROW` is a check on **one migration's own effect**, evaluated at the foot of its own `Up` inside
  its own transaction, before any later migration runs;
- it is **not a standing constraint on the tables**, and cannot see what a successor does;
- it fires if a convenience backfill is ever added **to that file**, which is what it was built to catch.

**So the ordering is the design:** the plane is created empty and *proved* empty, and a separate, dated,
reviewable migration fills it. Collapsing the two would have destroyed the evidence that made the fill
reviewable. Said in both migration files and in the seed, so a reader hitting any one of the three is told
about the others.

**Asserted, not only described:** `The_commercial_plane_migration_itself_creates_no_rows` and
`The_following_migration_seeds_the_trial_plan_without_tripping_that_throw` are the pair.

## Judgement calls, both ruled by the architect

**The seed writes the four `ModuleDefinitions`.** Not strictly required — nothing reads that table and
there is no foreign key. It is the only place in the database that says what the four granted keys mean
and which are gateable (`REQ-SUB-0013`), and a plan granting four bare names against an empty catalog
cannot be rendered in the plan list this trial is designed to sit in.

**`Down` is deliberately empty.** A data seed is not a schema operation and its rows are append-only
history; a `Down` deleting them would be the exact mutation `PreventAppendOnlyMutation` refuses, and could
not tell a trial a tenant still holds from one they have since moved off. Reverting past
`AddSubscriptionCommercialPlane` drops the tables and takes them along — the only coherent reversal.

**No status filter on `Tenants`.** Suspended and archived tenants are seeded too: `OD-SUB-0010` ruled
subscription state and `TenantStatus` orthogonal, and filtering here is that coupling — with the failure
landing on the tenant reactivated a year from now, found by its owner rather than by us. Asserted.

## `DEC-L-040` — the model cannot say "billed in no currency"

`TenantSubscription.BillingCurrencyCode` is mandatory: `nchar(3)`, non-null, three letters enforced in the
domain. A free trial is billed in nothing.

**Reported rather than fixed**, the same restraint the `IsTrial` temptation got. No nullable column, no
private sentinel — **ISO 4217 `XXX`**, the standard's own code for "no currency involved". It passes the
existing validation unchanged and reads as "no money" rather than as a bug.

**The 0 `XXX` price row is load-bearing, not decoration:** `REQ-SUB-0023` requires the plan to carry a
price in the tenant's billing currency and the assigning caller is meant to check it. Without that row the
seeded subscription violates an invariant on day one.

### On `DEC-L-015` and `DEC-L-038` — a 51-minute gap, and a different cause from the first two

**Third miss of the 20-minute rule in one day**, between `[14:54]` and `[15:46]` — two reports missed, not
one, and surfaced by the owner for the third time. Recorded because three misses of one rule in one day is
a finding about the rule rather than about the day.

**The cause is not the one the first two had, and it is also not the one I first reported.** Both of my
own accounts of it were wrong, and the second was falsified by evidence that arrived after I had already
sent it. The record carries the corrected version.

**What I reported at 15:47:** that the waiter I started at 14:56 — an `until [ -f gate.exit ]` loop
carrying a ten-minute tool timeout against a leg that needed sixty — had been killed at that timeout and
its notification never arrived.

**What actually happened:** that task **completed normally at 15:49**, having run for fifty-three minutes,
printing `=== 15:49 gate finished ===` and exiting 0. It was never killed. The ten-minute value did not
bound it.

**So the real cause is simpler and worse.** I started a wait sized to the thing being waited for — the
whole gate — rather than to the interval I owed a report on, and then ended my turn with no other
wake-up. There was no dropped signal and no dead timer. **I chose a fifty-three-minute silence and it was
delivered exactly as ordered.**

**`DEC-L-038` still could not have caught it, and the reason generalises:** a timestamp is written by the
turn that happens, and has nothing to say about the turn that never starts. Every mechanism aimed at
conduct *during* a turn is structurally unable to address a failure whose entire content is that there was
no turn.

**And the mechanism below is unaffected — it is the one thing all three accounts point at.** Whether the
waiter died or ran to completion, the defect is that its duration was set by the gate instead of by the
rule. That is what `DEC-L-042` forbids, and the corrected cause supports it more directly than my wrong
one did.

**Mechanism now in force: no wait may be longer than the reporting interval, whatever it is waiting for.**
Every background wait is a bounded poll capped at 13 minutes that then prints state and exits; its
completion notification *is* the report trigger. If the work is unfinished I report progress and start
another. Three properties:

1. the timer is never longer than the rule, so a late report is impossible by construction;
2. the harness is the clock rather than me, so it survives being absorbed;
3. a wait that goes wrong now costs at most one interval instead of fifty-one minutes.

**Its honest limit, stated rather than found later:** it fails if a turn ends without starting a waiter at
all. It makes waiting safe; it does not make starting automatic. That hole is not closable from inside a
turn, and saying so is better than a fourth fix carrying the first three's defect in silence.

**One correction to the charter detail this produced.** I told the architect that setting the tool timeout
well above the loop's own duration was what made the mechanism real, on the theory that a waiter killed by
its own timeout sends no notification. **The evidence contradicts that specific claim** — a
`run_in_background` task with a ten-minute timeout ran fifty-three minutes and completed. Keeping the
timeout generous is still right, but it is prudence rather than the load-bearing part; **the load-bearing
part is the bound on the loop itself.**
