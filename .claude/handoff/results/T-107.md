# 1. THE UNIT OF `WorkedQuantity` — THE ANSWER IS SHARPER THAN "UNASSERTED"

## Payroll never reads it. Not once.

```
grep WorkedQuantity across src/Modules/Payroll and tests/Payroll.Tests   ->   ZERO hits
PayrollEmployeeInput takes exactly two attendance fields:
    IReadOnlyDictionary<string, decimal>? OvertimeQuantityByTier
    decimal UnpaidAbsenceQuantity
```

**`WorkedQuantity` is recorded, summed, persisted and exposed on the wire, and no consumer exists.** It has
no unit because nothing has ever had to know one.

**So HOURLY and DAILY would be its FIRST consumer, and would have to ESTABLISH the unit rather than discover
it.** There is no existing behaviour to stay consistent with — which is easier than the alternative and is
the single most useful thing this establish found.

## And the two quantities Payroll DOES read are consumed in DIFFERENT UNITS from the same record

```
OvertimeQuantity        -> HOURS   "Attendance says three hours at tier NIGHT"     (AttendanceRecord.cs:154)
UnpaidAbsenceQuantity   -> DAYS    "the daily rate multiplied by the unpaid days"  (PayElement.cs:110)
```

**Both units are stated in prose comments and asserted nowhere.** No validation, no test, no schema
constraint, no criterion. A sweep of `docs/`, `tests/` and `src/` for any statement binding a quantity to a
unit returns **nothing**.

`AttendanceRecord` is per-DAY (it carries `AttendanceDate`), so a day's `WorkedQuantity` is *naturally*
hours-within-that-day — **but that is inference, and inference is what produces a silently wrong number.**

**You were right that this is the crux. An hourly rate multiplied by a day count is a payroll defect nobody
would see, and today nothing in the product would stop it.**

# 3. WHAT EACH TYPE BREAKS — YOUR THREE CONFIRMED, PLUS TWO YOU DID NOT NAME

### Your three, all real

**Calendar-day proration must not extend.** `ProrationFactor` (`:250-269`) is employed-days ÷ period-days.
For DAILY and HOURLY, rate × quantity **already** reflects what was worked; multiplying by the factor pays a
mid-month joiner a fraction of what they actually earned. **`OD-PAY-0015` ruled proration for the monthly
model and must not silently extend.**

**The unpaid-absence deduction is monthly-specific, and worse than you said.** `baseAmount / periodDays *
UnpaidAbsenceQuantity` divides by **calendar days** — against an hourly base that divisor is not merely
redundant, it is meaningless. For HOURLY, absence is hours not worked and there is nothing to deduct.

**CONTRACT is structural, and it collides with the aggregate's central decision:**

```
EmployeeCompensation.cs:22
  "There is no `EffectiveToUtc` and no `IsCurrent` flag. The end of one record is the start of the next."
```

**A one-time payment has no next record, so it would recur in every period forever.** That is not a missing
field; it is the design premise of the aggregate. **Saying what it would need, not inventing it:** either a
paid-once marker on the record, or CONTRACT is **not a compensation type at all** but a one-off pay
instruction — a different aggregate with a different lifecycle. **The second reading is cleaner and I am not
going to act on it without a ruling.**

### Two you did not name

**TRAP 4 — the grade band flags every hourly employee, permanently.** `WasOutsideGradeBand` is
caller-supplied and informational (`OD-PAY-0004` ruled recorded-and-warned, never refused). An hourly rate of
50 compared against a monthly band of 5000–10000 is outside it — and `:79-81` says the observation is
**stored rather than recomputed**, deliberately, so *"the honest record is what was true when the amount was
set."* **A wrong warning would be frozen into history by a rule designed to protect history.**

**TRAP 5 — the percentage behaviours change meaning.** `PercentageOfBaseSalary` computes off `baseAmount`.
If HOURLY makes `baseAmount` rate × hours, **a 10% allowance silently becomes a function of hours worked.**
That may be exactly right or exactly wrong — **it is a ruling, not a detail**, and it is invisible unless
someone asks.
