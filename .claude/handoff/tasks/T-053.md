## Scope

Sales domain model and the application layer over it:

- **Customer**, **sales order**, **sales invoice**, and the transitions between them.
- Pricing, discount and tax **as domain logic**, unit-tested without a database. Money arithmetic
  and rounding get explicit tests; a rounding rule that is only implied by a passing total is not
  tested, it is coincidental.
- **Application handlers** for the commands and queries the module owns.
- **`SSAS.Sales.Contracts`**: only what a consumer outside the module needs, nothing speculative.

Read `docs/00-Master-Product-Specification/Business-Rules.md` first and cite the rules you

## The dependency you do not have

Sales normally consumes **Inventory**, which **does not exist in this repo** and is not in scope.

Do not stub it, do not fake it, and do not build a private inventory inside Sales. Model the
boundary as an **explicit unmet dependency**: define in `SSAS.Sales.Contracts` the narrow port
Sales actually needs from stock, implement nothing behind it, and let the domain be testable
against a test double. State plainly in your result which behaviours are unimplementable without
it.

**Scope the order and invoice work to what is genuinely independent of stock.** An invoice for a
service line does not need inventory; a delivered goods line does. If that means Sales lands
narrower than its name suggests, land it narrower and say so — a module that silently pretends to
cover goods movement is worse than one that declares the gap.
