## What it asserts

`tests/API.Tests/Infrastructure/TenantModelResidencyTests.cs`:

1. **`No_platform_resident_type_is_in_the_model_the_cutover_copies_from`** — fifteen named
   Platform-resident types are absent from `ITenantModelSource.Model`, resolved from the real Host.
2. **`The_resolved_model_is_the_populated_one_and_two_platform_types_are_in_it_deliberately`** — the
   control.

**The control is the load-bearing half.** `FindEntityType` returns null for a type that is absent
*and* for a model that is empty, so fifteen nulls prove nothing on their own — the guard would keep
passing if the resolved model were ever replaced by an unconfigured one. `Branch` and `Company` are
Platform-Domain types that are in this model **deliberately**, so asserting their **presence** proves
the model is populated and that `FindEntityType` separates the two cases **on the same assembly the
absences are drawn from**.

**The list is fifteen types in two planes:** the seven commercial types, and eight identity/access
types. Every one carries a `TenantId` and therefore looks tenant-owned, which is what makes each a
decision rather than an obvious case.

**`TenantUser` is the entry the proxy guard could not express at all.** It is the only one that
actually carries `ITenantOwnedEntity`, so asserting the absence of that interface would simply fail on
it.

**It does not restate the tenant model's inventory.** H9 asserts that, and two lists of one thing
drift.
