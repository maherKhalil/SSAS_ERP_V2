# THE THINGS WORTH A PERSON'S ATTENTION

## 1. The ordering, and why the tests are about failures rather than the happy path

**Termination is terminal — verified, not assumed.** Every operation on `Employee` refuses once
`Terminated`; `Deactivate` requires `Active`; `Activate` requires `Inactive`; `ApplyTransition` has no path
out. **Nothing in the product can un-terminate an employee.**

So the two naive orders are not comparable:

| order | failure leaves | repairable |
|---|---|---|
| commit termination -> deactivate | terminated employee, live account | **no** |
| deactivate -> commit termination | active employee, closed account | yes, and the repair ships here |

The first **recreates `AC-SS-0012`'s exposure inside the change that closes it.**

**What shipped holds the tenant transaction open across the Platform write**, so the COMMON failures —
Platform unreachable, concurrency conflict, already-deactivated — all land before the commit and roll back
to exactly where the system started.

**`A_failed_closure_refuses_the_termination_and_rolls_it_back` is the assertion that distinguishes the
shipped order from the refused one.** It asserts `Commits == 0`, not merely a non-200: a handler that
committed first and reported the failure afterwards would pass a weaker test.

## 2. The cost is named where the transaction is opened, not only here

**An open tenant transaction now spans a cross-database call.** A slow Platform write holds a lock on that
`Employee` row for its duration — bounded by the command timeout, one row, not hot, and belonging to the
employee being terminated.

**A future reader who finds a remote call inside a transaction and no explanation will assume it is a
mistake.** The handler says it is a ruling and says what the alternative was.

## 3. `EmployeeTerminated` does not dispatch on the rollback path — confirmed

`EfUnitOfWork.SaveChangesAsync` dispatches **only when no transaction is open**; with one open, dispatch
happens inside `CommitAsync` *after* the commit, and the catch path rolls back and rethrows without
dispatching. **A domain event announcing a termination that did not happen is not reachable.**

## 4. The half-state is reported, not compensated

**A best-effort reactivate that itself failed would put us here with more code; one that silently succeeded
would hide that a write failed.** So `employee.termination_incomplete` names the state and the repair.

**The catch is deliberately broad, cancellation included** — a cancelled commit leaves exactly the same
half-state as a failed one, and letting it propagate would surface as a generic failure that says nothing
about what needs repairing. The narrow catch is the tidier code and the worse answer.

## 5. The interesting deactivator tests are the successes

**An employee with no account must still terminate, and today that is EVERY employee** — nothing in
production writes a `UserEmployeeLink` (T-092). Because the caller holds an open transaction, a spurious
failure here would not merely log: **it would make termination impossible.**

Already-deactivated is also a success, which is what makes the operator's retry after the half-state safe.

## 6. `Inactive` does not close the account, asserted rather than trusted

Deactivating an employee is unpaid leave or suspension — reversible, and T-090 resolves such an employee
normally. **Closing the account there would lock someone out of their own payslips while still employed.**
The two operations sit next to each other in one file and are one copy-paste apart, so it is a test.

## 7. A permission no route requires — two instances closed, the general case left alone

`Platform.Users.Deactivate` and `Platform.Users.Reactivate` were catalog-defined, grantable, and required by
no endpoint. **A grant that looks effective and authorizes nothing — the exact mirror of FP-006P.**

`EndpointPermissionCatalogJoinTests` asserts endpoints against the catalog; **nothing asserts the catalog
against endpoints.** T-091 closes these two by building their routes. The general case is the architect's
and is deliberately not widened into here.

## 8. Two stale claims corrected (`DEC-L-073`)

- **`UserEmployeeResolver`** said it was *the one read over `UserEmployeeLink`*. It is now the
  **user -> employee** read; `TenantUserDeactivator` reads **employee -> user** on the other unique index.
- **The Integration fixture's no-op deactivator** says so, and says where the real behaviour is asserted —
  a silent no-op in a fixture is how a guard stops being tested without anything turning red.
