### Administration is structural, not careful

`OD-SUB-0013` ruled platform-plane only, and the document states it as **a permission that does not
exist** — there is no tenant-plane name to grant by mistake, so a tenant administrator holding
everything the product defines still cannot change what their tenant pays for.

**It has to be structural**, because otherwise `BR-PLT-0008` would be enforced against a value the
enforced party controls, and `CON-0001` would rest on nobody granting the wrong permission.

Six new **platform-plane** permissions beside `Platform.Support.Administer`, the only one that exists
today. Coarse `View`/`Administer`, following that precedent rather than HR's per-act verbs.

### ⚠ A naming hazard I found while writing them, and want your eye on

**The `Platform.` prefix does not tell you which plane a permission belongs to.**
`Platform.Users.View` and `Platform.Roles.Create` live in `PlatformPermissionNames`, are granted to
**tenant** roles, and are enforced with `RequirePermission`. `Platform.Support.Administer` is
**platform**-plane. Same prefix, different planes — **only the convention method at the route
distinguishes them.** My six names extend the platform-plane set, so the ambiguity grows.

I **kept the prefix** and named the alternative, because `Platform.Support.Administer` established it
and a second scheme for seven permissions is worse than one ambiguous one. **The mitigation is
mechanical rather than a convention anyone must remember**: an architecture guard asserting that
every `Platform.Plans.*`, `Platform.Subscriptions.*`, `Platform.EntitlementGrants.*` and
`Platform.Invoices.*` name is used **only** with `RequirePlatformPermission`, never with
`RequirePermission`. `REQ-SUB-0004` is exactly what that guard protects; without it the requirement
is enforced by careful reading.

**If you would rather have an unambiguous prefix, it is cheap now and expensive after the permissions
are assigned.** Flagged for that reason, not to reopen the ruling.

### The two deferred requirements, closed

### One gap in the ruling set, named rather than guessed

**`OD-SUB-0017` ruled "seats plus limits" but did not rule what happens when a seat cap is
exceeded.** Both readings are consistent with the words:

- **Blocks** — the next login beyond the cap is refused, like a licence.
- **Bills** — the overage is metered and invoiced, and nobody is locked out.

These are very different products, and the difference lands on a user at the moment they try to work.
`BR-SUB-0006` states how the quantity is *judged* — against the record in force when observed — and
**deliberately does not state the consequence.** `business-rules.md` names it as a rule that cannot
be written yet. **This is the one gap I think a build could stumble into**, because "seats plus
limits" reads as complete until someone has to implement the enforcement branch.

Related and smaller: **no grace period is ruled.** `OD-SUB-0009` says expiry blocks login; whether
there is a window before it bites is unauthored, and a grace period would be a second ruling.
