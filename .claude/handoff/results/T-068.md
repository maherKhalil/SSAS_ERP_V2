## Three sites, classified by what happens today

### 1. Payroll base proration — CORRECT BY CONVENTION, AND UNVERIFIABLE

`PayrollCalculator.ProrationFactor` (`:247`) is `employedDays / periodDays`, derived from `HiredUtc`
against the period. **There is no other factor.** A part-timer employed all month gets `1.0`, and
`baseAmount = Compensation.BaseAmount * 1.0`.

**That is right if and only if the tenant entered a reduced `BaseAmount`.** The product has no opinion
and takes no ratio, so a part-timer's pay is correct exactly when a human encoded the ratio into the
salary figure.

**Nothing records that they did, nothing enforces it, and nothing can tell the two cases apart.** A
0.6 FTE employee on a full-time salary figure and a full-time employee are **identical to every query
in the system.**

### 2. Unpaid-absence deduction — WRONG ANSWER, SILENT, AND PROPORTIONAL TO THE RATIO

`PayElement.UnpaidAbsenceDeduction` is derived: **daily rate × `UnpaidAbsenceQuantity`**, where the
daily rate uses *"the SAME calendar-day divisor as proration"* — a decision, not a convenience
(`OD-ATT-0015`, owner-ruled).

For a full-timer that convention is internally consistent, which is the whole point of the ruling.
**For a part-timer it is not.** Someone working three days a week loses `1/30` of a month's pay for
missing a day that is `1/13` of their working month. **They are under-deducted, by a factor that is
exactly their unknown ratio.**

**Nothing announces it.** The payslip adds up, every line is defensible, the deduction is present, and
the figure is wrong. This is the shape the task named: *the wrong answer is the expensive case and the
one that will not announce itself.*

### 3. Leave consumption — WRONG ANSWER, SILENT, AND IMMUTABLE

`LeaveCommandHandlers` computes `calendar.WorkingDaysBetween(start, end)` against the **company**
calendar (`NoCalendarForCompany` — there is no per-employee calendar) and stores the figure on the
request:

```
// COMPUTED HERE, STORED ON THE REQUEST, NEVER RECOMPUTED.
```

**A part-timer taking a calendar week is charged five days when they work three.**

And the storage decision — right for its own reason, `AC-ATT-0019`, so a later holiday cannot alter a
past request — means **a future fix does not repair history.** Every leave request booked before
employment type exists keeps its wrong number permanently. **That makes this the only one of the three
that gets more expensive with time rather than merely staying wrong.**

### 4. Leave entitlement — DOES NOT EXIST

No `EntitlementDays`, no `AccrualRate`, no balance model anywhere in Attendance. **The concern is ahead
of the product**: leave is *consumed* and recorded, never *granted*. Pro-rating an entitlement by
employment type is not a gap in a thing that exists — it is a property of a thing that does not.

## What would need to ask, and cannot

The task's second instruction, and the answer is three closed doors:

- **`WorkingCalendar` is company-scoped.** There is no per-employee schedule for a pattern to attach
  to. A part-timer's working days have nowhere to live.
- **`AttendanceSummaryResult` carries quantities and nothing else** — *"No money, no rate, no
  multiplier"* (`DEC-ATT-0004`), deliberately. Payroll cannot learn a ratio through the existing
  contract without widening a boundary that was drawn on purpose.
- **`PayrollEmployeeInput` carries `Compensation` and `HiredUtc`.** No ratio, no schedule.

**Each of those is a good decision.** None of them is the obstacle on its own. The obstacle is that the
concept has no home in any of the three, and the module boundaries are drawn such that **any of the
three could ask and none of them can answer.**

## Three shapes wearing one name, and they are not derivable from one another

```
a RATIO      payroll                    what fraction of a full salary, and of a full day
a SCHEDULE   attendance                 which days, which hours
a POLICY     leave, benefits, access    what accrues; whether a contractor is an employee at all
```

**0.6 FTE does not tell you which days.** Which days does not tell you the pay ratio — a compressed
four-day full-time week is 1.0 pay on 4 days. **Neither tells you whether a contractor accrues leave**,
which is the question the owner actually asked and is a policy rather than an arithmetic.

**Reporting them as one number would hide exactly what makes the concept expensive**, which the task
predicted and the sites above confirm: site 1 needs the ratio, site 3 needs the schedule, and site 4
needs the policy. **A single `EmploymentType` enum satisfies none of them.**
