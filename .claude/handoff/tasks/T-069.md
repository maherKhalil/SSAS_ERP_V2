# T-069 — Make the full-time assumption announce itself

**Issued under `DEC-L-064` without a check-back on the decision to issue it. Check back on the
mechanism — see the last section.**

## What T-068 established, and why this follows

Three computations assume every employee is full-time. **None of them says so**, and two produce
silent wrong answers for anyone who is not:

| Site | Today | On the day employment type exists |
|---|---|---|
| `ProrationFactor` = `employedDays / periodDays` | correct **by convention** — a human encodes the ratio into `BaseAmount` and nothing records that they did | must take a ratio |
| Unpaid-absence deduction | **wrong, silent** — three-days-a-week loses `1/30` of a month for `1/13` of their working month | must take a schedule |
| Leave day-count | **wrong, silent, and immutable** — computed once, stored on the request, never recomputed (`AC-ATT-0019`, and correct for its own reason) | must take a schedule |

**The product is pre-release** — V1 is unimplemented and FP-014 is specified but not built — **so no
wrong record exists yet.** That is the whole reason this is worth doing now rather than urgently:
the third site's cost **accrues from first use**, and nothing has used it.

## What to build

**Guards that fail the moment the assumption stops being true**, at each of the three sites.

Today they cannot detect a part-time employee, because nothing in the model can. **So do not try to
detect one.** Assert the *assumption* instead: that the computation has exactly the inputs it has,
and that those inputs cannot express a ratio, a schedule, or a policy.

**The property to achieve:** when someone adds employment type to the model, **these three fail and
name what must change.** Not a comment. Not a `TODO`. A red test with a message that says *this
computation assumes full-time and the model can now express otherwise.*

**Each guard carries the site's own consequence in its failure message**, in T-068's terms — under-
deduction for site 2, and for site 3 that **every record already written keeps its wrong count
forever**, which is the one a reader will not infer.

## The one thing to check back on

**T-038 exists because name-based absence guards are a known weakness in this repository** — a guard
that greps for a type name passes vacuously the day someone spells it differently.

**So the mechanism is a design question and I want your judgement before you build it.** A guard
asserting the shape of a computation's inputs is stronger than one asserting a name is absent, but I
do not know what is reachable from the test surface without a database. **Bring me the shape.**

If the honest answer is that only a name-based guard is reachable, **say so** — a weak guard that
declares its weakness is worth more than a strong-sounding one that does not, and it is still worth
more than the comment there is today.
