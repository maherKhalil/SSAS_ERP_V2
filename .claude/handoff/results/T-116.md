# ⚠ AND THE ARITHMETIC SURFACED SOMETHING I DID NOT GO LOOKING FOR

**900 is `100 × (11 − 2)`. The two unpaid absence days are recorded on 20 January — five days AFTER this
employee was terminated — and they still deducted.**

```
working days   CLAMPED to the employment window        11, correct
unpaid days    NOT clamped — the summary's period total  2, from a date after termination
```

**T-115 bounded the working-day count and left the absence quantity unbounded.** `GetForPeriodAsync` sums
every attendance record for the employee in the PERIOD; nothing asks whether the employee was employed on
the day the absence was recorded.

**I am not calling this a payroll defect, and here is why I am unsure:** an attendance record dated after an
employee's termination arguably should not exist at all, which would make this Attendance's data-integrity
question rather than Payroll's arithmetic. **The number is right whenever attendance lies inside employment,
and wrong only when it does not.**

**Reported rather than fixed.** It is a ruling about which module owns the invariant, and the cost of
guessing is another expression that is right for the wrong reason.
