## The memory band — what was actually wrong with it

| Run | Leg | `min_free` | peak testhost |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-014 | Debug | 1,510 MB | 586 MB |
| T-014 | Release | 1,232 MB | 641 MB |
| **T-015** | **Debug** | **551 MB** | 564 MB |
| **T-015** | **Release** | **580 MB** | 573 MB |

**All four green.** T-016 recorded "1,200–1,500 MB" as the healthy band from the first two; the next
run went to **less than half that** on the same suite and the same box a few hours later.

So the band was never a property of the suite — **it was two samples generalised into a rule**, and the
note now says that about itself. It tells the reader to treat 550 MB as *the lowest seen so far*
rather than the floor of safe operation, and **to expect a future run to go lower without that meaning
anything**.

That last clause matters more than the corrected numbers. Replacing one band with a tighter band would
have reproduced the same defect with better data — the next low reading would look alarming again. The
fix is to say the range is observational and that **the TRX, not the number, is the check.**

**The false alarm is recorded with its arithmetic rather than as an anecdote:** the 1,946 MB reading
flagged as a risk was **nearly four times the lowest healthy figure since measured**, and both legs of
that very run went lower and finished green.

**The floor is untouched — verified after the edit:** `MEMORY_FLOOR_MB` remains 2,048 LEAN / 4,096
FULL. This corrects the documentation of *observed depth*, which is a different thing from the
*precondition*, and conflating them is what caused the alarm in the first place.
